# Bible Quiz Dataset Editorial Guide

> *The authoritative editorial standard for WhoAmI Bible character datasets.*

---

# 1. Introduction

This guide defines the editorial standards for the WhoAmI Bible Quiz datasets.

Its purpose is to ensure that every dataset provides a fair, enjoyable, and scripturally accurate experience, regardless of the contributor.

These standards exist to produce datasets that are:

- Biblically accurate
- Consistent in style and difficulty
- Fun to play
- Easy to review
- Easy to maintain

The goal of the dataset is not to reward obscure trivia or hidden facts.

Instead, it rewards players who carefully read and understand the Bible. Great clues encourage players to connect people, events, genealogies, chronology, and narrative details that they may have overlooked before.

A successful clue creates an "Aha!" moment, a brief pause where the player realises they already know the answer, even if it is not immediately obvious.

Every editorial decision in this guide exists to create more of those moments.

---

# Translation Standard

The WhoAmI Bible Quiz datasets use the **New World Translation (NWT)** as the primary English Bible reference.

This choice was made for editorial consistency and because the NWT uses modern, clear language that is well suited to quiz content.

The choice of translation should not be interpreted as an endorsement of a particular denomination or theological viewpoint. The datasets aim to represent the biblical text accurately within the chosen translation.

### How the NWT is Used

The NWT is primarily used for:

- Direct quotations where wording matters.
- Character, place, and object names.
- Verse citations used to support clues.

Most clues should **not** quote Scripture directly. Instead, contributors should summarise biblical events in natural language while remaining faithful to the cited passages.

### Future Datasets

This editorial guide is translation-agnostic.

Future datasets may be produced using other translations or source texts, provided that:

- a single translation is used consistently within a dataset;
- direct quotations faithfully match that translation; and
- names and terminology remain internally consistent.

Regardless of translation, the editorial principles in this guide remain unchanged.

# Editorial Principles

The quality of a dataset is measured by far more than factual correctness.

A clue can be completely true and still be a poor clue.

Likewise, a clue can come from a little-known verse and still be an excellent clue if it rewards understanding of Scripture.

Throughout this guide, remember the following principles.

## Scripture is the source of truth

Every clue must be supported by Scripture.

No clue should require external historical sources, traditional interpretations, or Bible dictionaries to be considered true.

If a reader cannot verify the clue from the cited passages, the clue should be rewritten or removed.

---

## Gameplay matters

The objective is not merely to teach facts.

The objective is to create an enjoyable guessing experience.

Every clue should gradually reduce the number of possible answers while remaining interesting to read.

A clue that is technically true but does little to narrow the possibilities is usually a weak clue.

Likewise, a clue that immediately gives away the answer should usually appear only in the Easy section.

---

## Reward understanding, not obscurity

Difficulty should never come from hiding information.

Instead, difficulty should reward players who understand Scripture well.

Good difficult clues often require players to connect passages, remember family relationships, recognise patterns, or understand the order of biblical events.

Poor difficult clues rely on details that almost nobody remembers.

---

## Prefer observation over interpretation

Whenever possible, describe what the biblical text actually says.

Avoid clues that depend upon a particular theological position or disputed interpretation.

For example, this is stronger:

> I began the journey to Canaan, but my son completed it.

than:

> I led my family toward the Promised Land.

The first describes the narrative.

The second introduces subtle theological language that does not appear in the cited passage.

---

## Accuracy is more important than cleverness

A memorable clue is valuable.

A clever clue is valuable.

Neither is worth sacrificing accuracy.

If a clue could reasonably be challenged by someone reading the cited verses, it should be rewritten or removed.

The strongest clues survive careful scrutiny.

---

## Every clue should teach something

Players should regularly finish a game having noticed something they had never appreciated before.

Examples include chronological observations, genealogical relationships, narrative patterns, repeated themes, and overlooked details.

A clue does not need to be obscure to accomplish this.

Often, the best clues simply present familiar information from a fresh perspective.

---

## Editorial mindset

Before writing any clue, ask the following questions:

- Does this reduce the number of possible answers?
- Is this memorable?
- Is this supported by the cited passages?
- Does it reward understanding rather than trivia?
- Could another careful Bible reader reasonably challenge it?
- Would I enjoy solving this clue myself?

If the answer to any of these questions is "no", reconsider the clue before adding it to the dataset.
