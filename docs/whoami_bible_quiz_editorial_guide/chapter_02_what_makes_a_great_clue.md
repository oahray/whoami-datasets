# 2. What Makes a Great Clue?

Every clue in a WhoAmI dataset serves a single purpose:

> To progressively narrow the list of possible answers while creating an enjoyable "Aha!" moment for the player.

A clue is not judged solely by whether it is true. It is judged by how well it contributes to the overall gameplay experience.

The best clues reward careful reading of Scripture rather than obscure memorisation.

---

## The Five Qualities of a Great Clue

Every clue should satisfy the following five qualities.

### 1. Accurate

A clue must be completely true according to the cited passages.

If the clue depends on disputed interpretations, assumptions, or external sources, it should be rewritten or removed.

Accuracy is the foundation upon which every other editorial decision is built.

---

### 2. Distinctive

A clue should meaningfully reduce the number of possible answers.

For example:

> I was the father of many children.

Although true of several biblical figures, this clue does little to narrow the possibilities.

Compare that with:

> I built an ark.

This immediately identifies a single character.

Even when writing Hard or Nightmare clues, contributors should ask:

> "Does this clue eliminate candidates?"

If not, it is probably not contributing much to the dataset.

---

### 3. Memorable

The best clues describe moments that readers remember.

These may be:

- significant events
- memorable conversations
- important relationships
- defining decisions
- unique characteristics

Players should often recognise the clue before remembering the answer.

For example:

> I began the journey to Canaan, but my son completed it.

This is memorable because it captures an important moment in Terah's life while rewarding players who understand the biblical narrative.

---

### 4. Progressive

No clue exists in isolation.

Each clue should contribute to a progression of difficulty.

Easy clues establish broad recognition.

Medium clues reinforce identity.

Hard clues require stronger familiarity.

Nightmare clues reward synthesis and careful observation.

Avoid placing an iconic clue in the Nightmare section simply because it appears in an obscure verse.

Likewise, avoid placing a highly inferential clue in the Easy section simply because it is technically true.

---

### 5. Enjoyable

Above all, clues should be satisfying.

A player should feel rewarded for recognising the answer.

Good clues create moments of discovery.

Poor clues create frustration.

If a clue feels like arbitrary trivia rather than meaningful biblical knowledge, it probably does not belong in the dataset.

---

# The "Aha!" Test

One of the simplest ways to evaluate a clue is to imagine a player reading it.

A strong clue usually produces this reaction:

> "Wait... I know this..."

followed shortly by recognition of the answer.

A weak clue often produces one of two reactions:

> "I've never heard that before."

or

> "That could describe lots of people."

Neither creates satisfying gameplay.

When reviewing clues, contributors should continually ask themselves:

> **Would this clue create an Aha! moment for a careful Bible reader?**

If the answer is no, consider rewriting or replacing it.

---

# Clues Are Part of a Story

A WhoAmI dataset should feel like a conversation with the player.

Each clue reveals another piece of the puzzle.

The player gradually builds a mental picture until the answer becomes obvious.

For this reason, contributors should avoid thinking of clues as isolated facts.

Instead, think of them as chapters in a story, each revealing just enough information to move the player one step closer to the correct answer.

A well-designed dataset leaves the player feeling that the answer was earned—not guessed.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Truth alone does not make a good clue.
- Every clue should reduce the candidate pool.
- Great clues are memorable and enjoyable.
- Clues should become progressively more challenging.
- The best clues reward understanding rather than obscure trivia.
- Every clue should contribute to an "Aha!" moment.
