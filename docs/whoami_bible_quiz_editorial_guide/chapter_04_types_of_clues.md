# 4. Types of Clues

Not all clues are created in the same way.

Some clues quote facts directly from Scripture.

Others require players to remember the flow of a biblical narrative.

The most rewarding clues ask players to connect multiple passages into a single observation.

Understanding these different clue types helps contributors build datasets that are varied, engaging, and progressively more challenging.

A balanced dataset typically contains several different clue types rather than relying on only one.

---

# Direct Clues

A direct clue is explicitly stated in Scripture.

The clue can be verified by reading the cited passage without requiring additional reasoning.

Examples:

> I was the wife of Moses.

> I built an ark.

> I had five daughters.

Direct clues form the foundation of most datasets.

They are usually appropriate for Easy and Medium difficulties, although a direct clue can occasionally be Hard if the fact itself is uncommon.

---

# Narrative Clues

Narrative clues reward players who remember the sequence of events in Scripture.

Rather than stating a fact directly, they describe something that happened during the biblical account.

Examples:

> I began the journey to Canaan, but my son completed it.

> My father brought me back to my husband in the wilderness.

These clues encourage players to think about the story instead of isolated facts.

Narrative clues often make excellent Medium and Hard clues.

---

# Relationship Clues

Relationship clues identify a character through their connections to others.

Relationships include:

- parents
- children
- spouses
- siblings
- descendants
- tribes
- kings
- prophets
- nations

Examples:

> I was the father of Isaac.

> I became the father-in-law of Moses.

Relationship clues are often among the easiest clues because many biblical characters are recognised through family connections.

However, more distant or unusual relationships can become Hard or Nightmare clues.

---

# Characteristic Clues

These describe something distinctive about the person rather than an event.

Examples include:

- occupations
- titles
- personality traits
- physical characteristics
- unique circumstances

Examples:

> I was the youngest of my brothers.

> I interpreted dreams.

> I was a tax collector.

Characteristic clues help create variety and prevent datasets from becoming simple retellings of biblical events.

---

# Chronological Clues

Chronological clues require reasoning about time.

The biblical text often provides enough information for readers to determine who lived during certain events or how different generations overlapped.

Examples:

> I was still alive when Sarah died.

These clues should only be used when the conclusion follows directly from the cited passages.

Do not rely on external chronological systems.

Chronological clues frequently make excellent Nightmare clues.

---

# Genealogical Clues

Genealogical clues reward players who understand biblical family trees.

Examples:

> Abraham was the ninth generation in line from me.

The genealogy should always be derivable from the cited passages.

Avoid requiring readers to consult external charts or study aids.

Genealogical clues are among the strongest forms of Nightmare clue because they reward careful reading rather than obscure memorisation.

---

# Comparative Clues

Comparative clues describe a character in relation to another person or event.

Examples:

> I was older than my brother.

> I died before my father.

> My son completed the journey that I began.

These clues often encourage players to think about relationships and chronology together.

Use them carefully to ensure they remain fully supported by Scripture.

---

# Synthesis Clues

Synthesis clues combine two or more passages into a single factual observation.

Unlike direct clues, the conclusion is not written as a single sentence anywhere in Scripture.

Instead, the player arrives at the conclusion by combining multiple biblical facts.

Examples:

> Abraham was the ninth generation in line from me.

> I was still alive when Sarah died.

Synthesis clues represent the highest level of editorial craftsmanship.

They should always be:

- objectively true
- fully supported by the cited passages
- based on a single reasonable conclusion

Many of the best Nightmare clues belong to this category.

---

# Choosing the Right Clue Type

No single clue type is inherently better than another.

Strong datasets combine several types.

For example:

Easy
- Direct
- Relationship

Medium
- Characteristic
- Narrative

Hard
- Narrative
- Comparative

Nightmare
- Chronological
- Genealogical
- Synthesis

This variety keeps the game interesting while rewarding different kinds of biblical knowledge.

---

# Editorial Balance

Avoid filling a dataset with only one kind of clue.

For example:

❌ Every clue describes a family relationship.

❌ Every clue describes an event.

❌ Every clue is chronological.

Instead, aim for a balanced mixture that tests different aspects of biblical understanding.

A varied dataset feels richer, more natural, and more satisfying to play.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Different clue types reward different kinds of biblical knowledge.
- Direct clues establish identity.
- Narrative clues reward remembering the biblical story.
- Relationship clues connect people together.
- Chronological and genealogical clues reward careful study.
- Synthesis clues are often the strongest Nightmare clues.
- A balanced dataset should include several different clue types.
