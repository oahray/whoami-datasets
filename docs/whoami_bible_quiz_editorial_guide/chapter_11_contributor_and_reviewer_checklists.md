# 11. Contributor and Reviewer Checklists

Even experienced contributors occasionally overlook details.

These checklists provide a quick way to verify that a dataset meets the project's editorial standards before it is submitted or approved.

They are not a substitute for careful review.

They are a reminder of the standards established throughout this guide.

---

# Contributor Checklist

Before submitting a dataset, confirm that you have completed each of the following.

## Biblical Accuracy

- ☐ Every clue is supported by Scripture.
- ☐ Every clue has been verified against the cited passages.
- ☐ No clue depends on disputed interpretation.
- ☐ Every inference leads to a single reasonable conclusion.

---

## Clue Quality

- ☐ Every clue is written in the first person.
- ☐ Every clue expresses one primary idea.
- ☐ Wording is natural and easy to read.
- ☐ No clue is unnecessarily vague or overly complicated.
- ☐ Every clue contributes something new.

---

## Difficulty

- ☐ Easy clues identify the character through well-known facts.
- ☐ Difficulty increases naturally throughout the dataset.
- ☐ Nightmare clues reward reasoning rather than obscure trivia.
- ☐ No clue feels significantly easier or harder than its assigned level.

---

## Variety

- ☐ The dataset includes a mixture of clue types.
- ☐ Relationships, narrative, chronology, genealogy, and characteristics are balanced where appropriate.
- ☐ No single clue type dominates the dataset.

---

## Citations

- ☐ Every clue includes supporting Scripture.
- ☐ Citations point directly to the supporting evidence.
- ☐ Multi-passage clues include all necessary references.
- ☐ No unnecessary citations have been included.

---

## Final Review

- ☐ I read the clues from beginning to end.
- ☐ The progression feels enjoyable.
- ☐ I removed weak or redundant clues.
- ☐ I would enjoy playing this dataset myself.

If any item remains unchecked, continue refining the dataset before submitting it for review.

---

# Reviewer Checklist

Reviewers should verify each dataset independently rather than relying solely on the contributor's work.

---

## Accuracy

- ☐ Every clue is scripturally correct.
- ☐ Every citation has been verified.
- ☐ Narrative inferences satisfy the Golden Rule.
- ☐ No assumptions have been introduced.

---

## Gameplay

- ☐ Candidate reduction improves throughout the dataset.
- ☐ Difficulty progression feels natural.
- ☐ Clues remain enjoyable to solve.
- ☐ The final clues produce satisfying "Aha!" moments.

---

## Editorial Consistency

- ☐ First-person perspective is maintained.
- ☐ Difficulty labels are appropriate.
- ☐ Clue wording matches project style.
- ☐ Citation style is consistent.
- ☐ Translation standards have been followed.

---

## Overall Quality

Ask yourself:

- Would I approve every clue in this dataset?
- Would I enjoy playing it?
- Does it meet the standards described in this guide?

If the answer to any of these questions is "no", provide clear, constructive feedback explaining why.

---

# Review Outcomes

Editorial review generally results in one of four outcomes.

### Approved

The dataset satisfies the editorial standards and is ready for publication.

---

### Approved with Minor Revisions

The dataset is fundamentally sound.

Only small wording, citation, formatting, or ordering adjustments remain.

---

### Revision Required

The dataset shows promise but requires meaningful editorial work before approval.

Examples include:

- inaccurate clues;
- weak progression;
- repetitive clue types;
- insufficient citations.

---

### Rejected

The dataset does not currently meet the project's editorial standards.

Rejection should be accompanied by clear explanations and practical guidance to help the contributor improve their work.

Whenever possible, reject clues rather than contributors.

The objective is always to improve the dataset.

---

## Editorial Insight

A checklist should never replace judgement.

It exists to prevent simple mistakes, not to discourage thoughtful editorial discussion.

If a contributor or reviewer believes a clue improves the dataset while technically falling outside a guideline, the reasoning should be documented and discussed.

The guide exists to promote consistency, but consistency should always serve the larger goals of biblical accuracy, enjoyable gameplay, and editorial integrity.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Use the contributor checklist before every submission.
- Use the reviewer checklist before every approval.
- Apply editorial judgement alongside the checklists.
- Explain feedback clearly and respectfully.
- The goal is always a better dataset.
